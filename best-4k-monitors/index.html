01

BEST PREMIUM PICK

ASUS ROG Swift OLED PG32UCDM3

For a premium setup that splits its time between creative work and games.

View on Amazon ↗ (opens in a new tab)

A bigger OLED picture, with speed to match.

The PG32UCDM3 puts a 4K image and a 240Hz refresh rate on a 32-inch QD-OLED panel. It is the Gen3 model, so check the full model number when shopping. ASUS lists DisplayPort 2.1, two HDMI 2.1 inputs and USB-C with 90W charging, making it the most flexible premium pick here for a desktop and laptop setup.

Tom’s Hardware’s measured review supports its high-end, mixed-use appeal. We would choose it for someone who values OLED contrast and gaming performance enough to pay well beyond the midrange. That is a different buyer from someone trying to stay under $400.

+ Why it earns its place
  • 4K detail and 240Hz in a spacious 32-inch display.
  • USB-C power plus HDMI and DisplayPort options.
  • ASUS lists a three-year warranty including panel burn-in.
− What to live with
  • By far the highest recorded price on this shortlist.
  • TFTCentral reported VRR flicker during its testing.
  • Some owners report coating scratches; these are anecdotes, not a measured failure rate.

Skip it if your budget is close to $400, or you want to avoid OLED maintenance for a desktop full of static editing panels.

02

BEST VALUE

AOC Gaming U27G4XM

The most convincing fit here for one monitor and a roughly $400 budget.

View on Amazon ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Put the budget into the screen you use every day.

AOC’s specifications pair a 27-inch 4K IPS panel with a 1,152-zone Mini-LED backlight. At full resolution it runs up to 160Hz; dual mode drops resolution to 1080p and raises the refresh ceiling to 320Hz. The lower-resolution mode can reduce the load on your GPU, with a clear loss of fine detail.

The recommendation rests on more than a high refresh number. Tom’s Hardware found an accurate sRGB mode and strong gaming value, while noting stand wobble, awkward button controls and no speakers. That combination makes it a useful starting point for web-oriented photo editing and games at its recorded $349.99 price.

+ Why it earns its place
  • 4K/160Hz for mixed creative work and gaming.
  • Mini-LED local dimming for better contrast than a basic IPS backlight.
  • A 1080p/320Hz option when speed matters more than detail.
− What to live with
  • No USB-C video or laptop charging.
  • A stand that can wobble and no OSD joystick.
  • Wide-gamut support alone does not guarantee the right presets for your editing workflow.

For photo work start with the measured sRGB mode. For an Adobe RGB or Display P3 workflow, check calibration and gamut-mode requirements carefully; the ProArt below provides dedicated creator presets.

03

BEST BUDGET PICK

Dell 27 Plus 4K S2725QS

For photo editing, everyday work and games that do not demand the fastest response.

View on Amazon ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Spend less. Keep the 4K detail.

The Dell makes sense when photo work and general desktop use come first. It combines a 27-inch 4K IPS panel with a 120Hz refresh rate, and WIRED’s testing found accurate color and full sRGB coverage. At the recorded $279.99 price, it is the least expensive main pick.

The gaming compromise deserves attention. RTINGS found slow response times and blurry motion. A 120Hz refresh rate cannot fix that on its own. For slower-paced games it can be a practical compromise; for competitive play, we would move up to the AOC.

+ Why it earns its place
  • Sharp 4K detail at the lowest recorded price here.
  • Accurate sRGB performance in independent testing.
  • 120Hz for a smoother desktop than 60Hz.
− What to live with
  • Motion blur in fast games.
  • No USB-C, USB hub or headphone socket.
  • Limited contrast and HDR performance.

Check the suffix S2725QS is this model. The similarly named S2725QC is a different, USB-C-equipped version.

05 / BEFORE YOU BUY

Make the screen fit your setup.

01

One monitor or two?

With $400 total, our recommendation is one capable screen. The ProArt alone uses almost all of that budget at its recorded price. Choose two screens when the extra desktop space matters enough to justify a larger total spend.

02

Still using an RTX 2060?

Keep 4K for your photos and desktop. For demanding games, adjust settings, use DLSS where supported, or lower the render resolution. The AOC’s 1080p mode is another option. A 320Hz mode does not mean your GPU will produce 320fps.

03

Which kind of photo editing?

For photos intended for the web, accurate sRGB is a useful priority. Adobe RGB print work or a Display P3 workflow needs more attention to gamut, calibration and color management. A large gamut percentage alone is not a complete answer.

↗

Check your connection before chasing refresh rates. The GPU output, monitor input, cable and display mode must support the combination you want. Check the exact model’s manual for supported timings.

A FEW PRACTICAL ANSWERS

Still weighing
your options?

The questions behind the spec sheet.

Is a 4K 60Hz monitor enough for photo editing?+

It can be. Photo editing does not require a high refresh rate, so color behavior, resolution and calibration may matter more. But if you also want smoother gaming on that same screen, the 60Hz ProArt is a deliberate compromise.

Does AOC dual mode keep 4K at 320Hz?+

No. The U27G4XM supports up to 160Hz at 4K or up to 320Hz at 1920 × 1080. The second mode trades detail for a higher refresh ceiling. Supported timings also depend on the connection and graphics hardware.

Should I worry about OLED burn-in when editing?+

Static toolbars and panels are a reason to consider your usage pattern. OLED can develop permanent image retention, so follow the manufacturer’s panel-care guidance and verify warranty terms. ASUS explicitly lists burn-in coverage for the PG32UCDM3. An IPS alternative avoids that particular OLED trade-off.

Will a new graphics card make the monitor a better buy?+

A faster GPU can help you use more of a high-refresh monitor’s capability, depending on the game and settings. It does not change the panel’s color accuracy, response behavior or ports. Buy for the work you do now and the gaming performance you can realistically reach.
